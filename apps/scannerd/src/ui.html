      Setup

      
        Where letters go
        
      
        Put a page where letters will lie and press Take a picture. The scanner finds the page and puts a box around it. Drag inside the box to move it, drag a corner to resize it, or draw a new one — then save. Only the area in the box is watched and photographed.
        
      
        
      
      
        The page looks like a trapezium rather than a rectangle. Drag each yellow corner exactly onto a corner of the page — the dashed lines should run along its edges. Every photograph is then straightened so that page comes out square, and whatever table shows beside a letter that lies a little off is trimmed away. Drag anywhere else in the picture to move all four.
        
      
        
      
        
        
        
        
          
          
        
        
        
        
        
      
        
      
        
        
        
      
        

      
        Which way up
        
      
        Turn the photographs until a page reads upright in the live view at the top. Pages photographed from now on are turned the same way; the picture above for setting the area stays as the camera sees it.
        
      
        
        
        
      
        
      
        

      
        Exposure
        
      
        White paper fools a camera into making it pure white and the text pale. Every photograph is checked; one that is too bright is taken again darker while the page is still there, and the next page is taken at what worked.
        
      
        
      
        

      
        Folders and people
        
      
        Once Paperless has read a letter, the display says which of these folders it goes in. Each folder is a Paperless tag of the same name, which the scanner creates. Give a folder a few words that only its letters contain — a number plate, an insurer, “Finanzamt” — and it works from the first letter; leave the words empty and Paperless learns from the documents you tag with it. Mark one as the bin for post that needs no paper copy. Tick Person for somebody in the household who gets post — their name, and any other spelling of it — and the display says who a letter is for as well as where it goes.
