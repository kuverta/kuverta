# People

**People** shows your mail the way a messenger does: a list of the people you
have mail with and, for each, your exchange as a conversation, with their
messages on the left and yours on the right.

![People: the list of correspondents, and one conversation in bubbles](../assets/screens/people-light.webp#only-light)
![People: the list of correspondents, and one conversation in bubbles](../assets/screens/people-dark.webp#only-dark)

Switch to it with **People** at the top right of the message list. **Messages**
switches back.

## The list

There is one row per person, most recent first: their name, how many messages
you have exchanged, how many of theirs are unread, the subject of the latest,
and its first line, which starts with "You:" when you wrote last. Type in the
search box to narrow the list to names and addresses.

A person is whoever sent you a message, and whoever you sent one to (the first
recipient). Mail you sent from any program counts, because it comes back
through your Sent folder. Newsletters, marketing and notifications are left
out, and **show bulk senders** brings them in. Mail in the Trash and Junk is
left out.

## A conversation

Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> or click a person. Their messages and yours are shown
oldest first, with the day between them and the subject whenever it changes.
Each bubble shows only what that message added. The quoted earlier message, the
"On … wrote:" line that introduces it, a forwarded original and the signature
are taken off. Unread messages have an amber edge. Double-click a bubble to
open the whole message in the ordinary view.

## Replying

Type under the conversation and press **Send** (or <kbd>⌘</kbd>/<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>
+<kbd>Enter</kbd>). It is sent as a reply to their latest message, so it keeps
the thread in their mail program, with their message quoted below yours. It
appears in the conversation at once, and the copy filed in Sent takes its place
at the next sync.

The keys that act on single messages (archive, delete, reply-all) belong to
the Messages view. In People they only say so.
